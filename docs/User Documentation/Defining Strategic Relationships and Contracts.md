# Defining Strategic Relationships and Contracts

Strategic Relationships and Contracts are used to define how organizational units are expected to work together from a strategy perspective.

Unlike the organizational hierarchy, which defines reporting and accountability, Strategic Relationships define alignment, influence, collaboration, and governance.

---

# Strategic Relationship vs Strategic Contract

## Strategic Relationship

A Strategic Relationship identifies **who is connected to whom** and **why**.

Example:

```text
Corporate → India
```

Relationship Type:

```text
Governance
```

Example:

```text
Europe ↔ North America
```

Relationship Type:

```text
Collaboration
```

---

## Strategic Contract

The Strategic Contract defines **how the relationship operates**.

A contract describes:

- Expected alignment
- Decision authority
- Required coordination
- Review cadence
- Governance expectations

Think of the relationship as the connection and the contract as the rules.

---

# Relationship Types

## Governance

Used when one organization provides strategic direction to another.

Example:

```text
Corporate → Consumer Division
```

Typical use cases:

- Corporate strategy alignment
- Strategic oversight
- Enterprise-wide initiatives
- Portfolio management

---

## Collaboration

Used when two organizations must work together.

Example:

```text
Europe ↔ North America
```

Typical use cases:

- Shared objectives
- Shared investments
- Shared capabilities
- Joint programs

---

## Dependency

Used when one organization depends on another.

Example:

```text
Manufacturing → Supply Chain
```

Typical use cases:

- Shared services
- Technology dependencies
- Operational dependencies
- Capability dependencies

---

## Oversight

Used when an organization requires visibility or direct involvement even though it may not sit in the reporting line.

Example:

```text
Corporate → India
```

Typical use cases:

- Emerging markets
- Strategic projects
- Turnaround programs
- High-risk operations

---

# Alignment Expectations

Alignment Expectations define which parts of strategy should align between the two organizations.

---

## Concerns

Defines whether strategic concerns should be aligned.

Examples:

```text
Growth
Supply Chain Risk
Sustainability
Customer Retention
```

Options:

```text
Required
Recommended
Optional
```

### Required

Organizations must address the same concern.

### Recommended

Organizations are encouraged to align.

### Optional

No alignment expected.

---

## Objectives

Defines whether strategic objectives should align.

Examples:

```text
Increase EBITDA
Grow Market Share
Reduce Cost
Improve Safety
```

Options:

```text
Required
Recommended
Optional
```

---

## Capabilities

Defines alignment expectations for business capabilities.

Examples:

```text
Supply Chain Management
Digital Commerce
Procurement
Operations Planning
```

Options:

```text
Required
Shared
Independent
```

### Shared

Both organizations jointly own or develop the capability.

### Independent

Each organization manages the capability separately.

---

## Investments

Defines how funding decisions are managed.

Examples:

```text
ERP Modernization
AI Program
Plant Expansion
```

Options:

```text
Parent Approval Required
Joint Approval
Independent
```

---

## Outcomes

Defines expected outcome alignment.

Examples:

```text
Revenue Growth
Production Throughput
Customer Satisfaction
```

Options:

```text
Required
Recommended
Optional
```

---

# Decision Rights

Decision Rights define who has authority.

This is often the most important part of the contract.

---

## Mandate

Can require a decision.

Example:

```text
Corporate mandates sustainability targets.
```

---

## Approve

Must approve prior to execution.

Example:

```text
Corporate approves major investments.
```

---

## Review

Reviews the decision but does not approve.

Example:

```text
Corporate reviews annual strategy.
```

---

## Consult

Input is required before decisions are made.

Example:

```text
Digital Office must be consulted.
```

---

## Inform

Must be notified.

Example:

```text
Regional leadership receives updates.
```

---

## Independent

Makes decisions without external approval.

Example:

```text
Business unit manages local initiatives independently.
```

---

# Review Cadence

Defines how often the relationship should be reviewed.

Options:

```text
Monthly
Quarterly
Semi-Annual
Annual
```

### Typical Guidance

| Relationship | Typical Cadence |
|-------------|----------------|
| Governance | Quarterly |
| Collaboration | Monthly or Quarterly |
| Dependency | Monthly |
| Oversight | Monthly or Quarterly |

# Guidance

When creating a relationship, ask:

1. Why do these organizations need to interact strategically?
2. Who influences whom?
3. What must align?
4. What can remain independent?
5. Who makes decisions?
6. How often should alignment be reviewed?

The goal is not to model reporting lines.

The goal is to explicitly model strategic influence, accountability, autonomy, and collaboration across the enterprise.
